Framing note for presenting this slide: lead time is supporting evidence for the calibrated-confidence claim, not the headline result on its own. Early Arm A testing showed Autoware's own MRM already reacts fast once something is sufficiently wrong, so a single "we beat MRM by N seconds" number has limited independent value — the actual point of the lead-time computation is the SHAPE of the curve (how lead time trades off against fault severity and against the calibration guarantee's tightness), which is a genuinely different kind of result than a single number, and it doesn't restrict the dissertation from moving beyond detection the way an isolated headline metric would.

Honest caveats to have ready, all already flagged in the artifacts themselves, not hidden: (1) even after tightening, achievable alpha is ~0.025-0.028, not the requested 0.1 — used 0.1 anyway since it's comfortably inside what's achievable now, but the guarantee's tightness is still gated by how many zone-tagged nominal trials exist; (2) the only fatal-moment ground truth right now is Arm A's own metrics.json heuristic — self-referential until Arm B data exists; (3) looking at the representative trace directly, this trial's residual signal actually crosses the threshold well before the zone-entry window the script currently searches from — meaning the reported ~102s lead time is a CONSERVATIVE lower bound, the true number is likely larger. Flagged as a real, not-yet-realized upside, not chased down yet per "see results before optimizing."

The discriminability refinement (negative-evidence subtype, not "TL faults in general") is a genuine finding worth defending on its own terms, but per Kalpit's own read: it's a sharpening of an existing claim, not a new contribution — good supporting material for the paper/dissertation background, not something to lead with as new research.
