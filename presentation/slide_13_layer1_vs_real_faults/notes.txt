The IMU-vs-TL distinction matters directly for how Layer 2 gets scoped
(slide 15) — a 3-second consequence-estimation horizon is well-matched to
TL's fast timescale and structurally cannot see IMU's 100+-second
compounding effect coming in time. This is presented here as a finding,
not yet as the Layer 2 design decision — that's slide 15's job.

The severity-sweep pilot result needs the confound explained honestly if
asked why it isn't cleaner: the existing fault variant used for this
first look ramps severity continuously WITHIN a single approach to an
intersection, so how severe the fault is and how close the vehicle is to
the actual decision point are entangled in that specific data. This
isn't a flaw in the finding, it's exactly the kind of thing careful
analysis is supposed to catch — and it directly justifies why the next
data collection (slide 15) uses a fixed-severity design instead.
