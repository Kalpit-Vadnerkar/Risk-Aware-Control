DOES THE CALIBRATED SIGNAL ACTUALLY NOTICE A REAL FAULT?

First time this whole project checked the calibrated model against LIVE
injected faults (not synthetic held-out perturbation) — across all 8
fault campaigns, all 20 available trials, every predicted feature.

Two genuinely different failure signatures found — not one:

(Plot: imu_fault_trace.png — ALL 7 predicted features, residual over time,
fault window shaded, all THREE calibration schemes overlaid: vanilla
(flat grey), Mondrian (stepped green, jumps at real zone boundaries), and
embedding (purple, tracks the residual's own shape closely — visibly the
most locally-adaptive of the three))

IMU faults corrupt STATE ESTIMATION. Residual triples during the fault
window, then KEEPS GROWING afterward instead of recovering — from 44%
exceeding the calibrated band right after the fault "ends" to 78% by
60-120 seconds later. A corrupted internal position estimate doesn't
self-heal once poisoned.

(Plot: tl_fault_trace.png — same full-feature, three-scheme layout)

TL faults corrupt a PERCEPTION VALUE. Far more contained — clean 4%,
active 17%, recovering to 24% shortly after. Real, but a fundamentally
different (much faster) timescale than IMU faults.

Worth a beat on its own: across BOTH plots, the embedding-conditioned
band (slide 12) visibly hugs the residual's own ups and downs far more
than vanilla's flat line or Mondrian's step function — a direct visual
of the same "improves automatically, adapts locally" property claimed on
slide 12, now shown under real fault conditions, not just nominal data.

The feature that carries the clearest evidence isn't the obvious one —
likely a genuinely new finding, not previously reported anywhere found in
the literature review:
  → IMU faults: ACCELERATION shows a 13.7x exceed-rate jump (vs.
    position's only 1.7x) — acceleration is closer to what IMU
    corruption directly touches; position is a laggier, downstream
    consequence
  → TL faults: VELOCITY beats the directly spoofed traffic-light
    features themselves — a miscalibrated light shows up more in the
    resulting driving behavior than in the model's residual on the
    signal that was actually spoofed

First look at a real severity relationship (Plot: tl_severity_dose_response.png):

Discovered that already-collected data (a confidence-decay fault variant)
contains ~26 independent severity sweeps never analyzed this way before.
traffic_light_confidence shows a clean, near-monotonic rise with severity
— a real, encouraging dose-response. Other features show a confound
(severity correlates with how far into the intersection approach the
vehicle is) — caught, understood, and already fixed in the design for
the next data collection round (slide 15).
