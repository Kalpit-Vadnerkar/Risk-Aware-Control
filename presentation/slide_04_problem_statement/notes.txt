This is the slide to be most precise on if the advisor pushes on scope.
The honest framing: Layer 1 is "almost low-key done" — the calibration
methodology, the audit that found a real gap, the training-level fix, the
conditional-calibration comparison, and a first real-fault validation are
all built and give a coherent story. What's NOT done yet within Layer 1:
a proper controlled severity sweep (in progress, concrete lab plan exists
— slide 15) and more nominal calibration data (currently only 7 held-out
trials, flagged as load-bearing-weak by three independent checks — say
this plainly, it's real, not modesty).

The two-paper split is a genuine scoping decision made this cycle, not a
hedge — an independent literature-review agent, asked to find fault with
standalone novelty if it could, verdicted Layer 1 alone as dissertation-
worthy without Layer 2 in the same paper (slide 3). Splitting lets each
piece be argued at full depth instead of compressing two non-trivial
contributions into one paper's space.

If asked "why not just do detection with a threshold, why does Layer 2
need reachability specifically" — because a raw anomaly magnitude can't
distinguish "the model is surprised on an empty road" (irrelevant) from
"the model is equally surprised near a pedestrian crossing" (potentially
severe) — that distinction is exactly what a physical consequence model
adds, and it's why Layer 2, not a bigger Layer 1, is the actual thesis
payload.
