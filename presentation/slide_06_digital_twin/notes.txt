Three additional calibration fixes worth mentioning if asked about model quality: (1) position was scaled relative to each window's own bounding box, which is invisible to the model and made a trivial constant-velocity baseline outperform the trained model by 30x — fixed by switching to a fixed-scale relative-displacement representation. (2) beta-NLL reweighting (Seitzer et al., ICLR 2022) fixed the standard Gaussian NLL's pathology of down-weighting gradients by 1/variance, which was preventing the model from learning to widen its variance appropriately at longer horizons. (3) a variance floor bug (VAR_FLOOR=1e-4) was pinning position's predicted uncertainty at exactly 1.0m regardless of input, once the representation fix shrank real error below that floor — lowered to 1e-8 and stress-tested against synthetic near-zero-residual data before retraining.

Current calibration status (z-score std, want 1.0): position 0.885 (mildly underconfident, and drifts to 1.177 at longer horizons), velocity 1.174, steering 1.101, traffic_light_state 1.332 (the most overconfident remaining, worth watching once conformal calibration is layered on top — that layer should catch and correct this by construction, per docs/research_notes/model_improvement_notes_2026.md, but it's confirmed empirically not just assumed).

Known, deliberately deferred limitations (see docs/research_notes/model_improvement_notes_2026.md, not hidden): route/goal information reaches the model via path_node graph flags but gets diluted by uniform mean-pooling over ~150 graph nodes; no deep ensemble yet (variance is aleatoric-only, no epistemic decomposition); VAR_FLOOR is one global constant, not per-feature; object encoder is mean-pool-only, not attention-weighted. None of these are blocking — logged as the honest punch list for later.
