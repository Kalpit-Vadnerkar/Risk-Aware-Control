This slide's most important beat is the abandoned-approach admission — it
is a real, useful part of the narrative, not a weakness to hide. It sets
up exactly why conformal calibration was chosen (a decisive calibration
win) while being honest that it did NOT win on point accuracy (mixed,
actually slightly worse on 4/6 features). If asked "why not just fix the
distributional head instead of abandoning it" — the pathology (dof-
collapse under heteroscedastic regression) is a documented, known failure
mode of jointly training mean+scale+dof in one objective, not a bug
specific to this implementation; conformal calibration sidesteps the whole
class of problem rather than patching one instance of it.

The per-head horizon embedding fix is a real, separate, already-verified
bug fix (found via direct gradient-attribution analysis, not guessed) —
worth a sentence if there's time, since it's concrete engineering evidence
of care taken, not hand-waving.

If asked about model size/compute: 507,249 parameters — small enough that
compute cost is not a constraint on any of this cycle's experiments
(training a full point-predictor variant takes under an hour).

Don't imply the whole model is "map-anchored" the way the old belief-
divergence slide risked — the map contributes as ROUTE/GRAPH CONTEXT via
the graph encoder for every feature, it is not a verification oracle for
any single channel the way the old traffic_light_discrepancy mechanism
was. That old, narrower claim is fully retired (slide 1/5).
