FINDING AND FIXING THE GAP AT THE MODEL LEVEL, NOT JUST RECALIBRATING

Systematic audit, grounded in real geometry (not guessed categories):
every calibration window labeled by proximity to real map zones — turn
zones, intersection zones, curved-road zones, open road — the SAME
geometry that gates fault injection (slide 8), reused rather than
invented.

The hidden gap, quantified: pooled steering coverage reads ~90% (looks
fine) — but splits into 94.2% on calm driving and only 75.6% near real
intersections. A single aggregate number cannot see this; the audit can.

The fix — retrain, don't just widen the interval:
  → Oversample turn/intersection-proximate windows during training,
    weighted by each window's OWN actual difficulty
  → Fully data-driven: weights are inverse-frequency-derived from
    whatever the CURRENT training set's distribution actually is — no
    hand-picked "make turns matter 3x more" constant to go stale as more
    data is collected

Why fix the model instead of just widening the calibrated band there:
widening the interval near intersections is statistically honest but
costs detection sensitivity exactly where faults are most often relevant
(slide 8's zone geometry is literally where faults are gated to occur) —
a wider-but-honest band there is a worse trade than a model that's
actually better at turns.

(Plot: v2_turn_scalar_bands.png, v2_turn_position_map.png — SAME sharpest-
turn window as slide 10, after the fix)

Directly compare to slide 10's v1 plot of the identical window: the
predicted trajectory now visibly hugs the actual curve, and steering/
lateral-velocity predictions clearly rise and peak with the real
maneuver instead of staying flat.

Validated, not just visually — intersection steering coverage: 75.6% →
87.3%. Position residual on the hardest turn cases: down 47%. Real,
measured, expected trade-off: calm-driving accuracy costs a few percent
(the model is now mildly biased toward expecting more curvature
everywhere) — an honest, worthwhile trade given what improved.
