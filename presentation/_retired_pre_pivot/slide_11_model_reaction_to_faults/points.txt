Discriminability (Plot: discriminability_rank_table.png) — this part still
holds, unaffected by the calibration finding:
  → Tests whether TL-related residuals are selectively dominant for TL
    faults vs. IMU faults (the mechanistic prediction from slide 5)
  → Result: NOT uniform dominance — velocity/steering residuals dominate
    for IMU faults specifically (physically direct: IMU feeds velocity/
    steering estimation)
  → traffic_light_discrepancy ranks #1 ONLY for tl_fault_s3/s4 — the two
    genuine negative-evidence campaigns (perception reports UNKNOWN/nothing)
  → A sharper, more defensible claim than "TL faults in general": the
    mechanism holds for the negative-evidence subtype specifically

Calibration tightening (Plot: calibration_tightening_table.png) — this was
conformal-threshold tightening, a different (now de-emphasized) calibration

NEW 2026-08-06 (Plots: nominal_floor.png, fault_reaction_grid.png) — what
the SPRT signal actually does, checked the way the reframe asks (is it
quiet under nominal noise, does its shape react sensibly to a fault):
  → p_fault_* saturates to >0.99 confidence roughly every 9 seconds during
    PURE NOMINAL driving with no fault present (92% of time at floor, but
    17.6 full reset-to-saturation events per trial on average)
  → Root cause traced, not just observed: 96% of that is the
    traffic_light_discrepancy branch alone — a ~9.3%-per-step nominal
    positive rate is too high for a memoryless sequential test to treat as
    rare evidence
  → Direct downstream consequence of slide 10's calibration finding, not a
    separate bug — fixing calibration is expected to fix this too
  → Fault-onset-aligned view (fault_reaction_grid.png) shows no clearly
    visible step-change at onset yet, as expected given the above — not
    evidence the underlying discriminability result (above) is wrong, just
    currently drowned out by this specific branch's noise floor
