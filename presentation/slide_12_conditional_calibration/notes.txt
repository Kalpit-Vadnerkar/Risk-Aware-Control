This slide exists specifically to avoid overselling either technique —
the "Mondrian isn't a free win" finding was caught by checking the SIZE-
WEIGHTED net effect before presenting the width comparison, not assumed;
the first draft of this result nearly presented Mondrian as an
unambiguous win before that check was done. Say this directly if it comes
up: it's a genuine example of catching an overclaim before it went out,
not an incidental detail.

If asked "which one should the dissertation actually use" — honest
answer, not yet decided: Mondrian is simpler, has an actual finite-sample
guarantee per group (not just approximately, the way the continuous
method does), and is the safer default. The continuous approach is more
elegant and compounds with future model improvements for free, but is
less mature (noisier, not yet monotonic in its one tuning parameter). This
is presented as live, ongoing methodology, not a settled choice — that's
accurate, not a hedge.

This slide is also where the standard statistical-prediction literature
gets cited most directly if asked for related work: Mondrian conformal
regressors (Boström & Johansson, 2020) for approach A; localized/kNN
conditional conformal prediction (Guan, 2023) as the principled version
of approach B's current ad hoc k-nearest-neighbor implementation — a
named next step, not yet built.
