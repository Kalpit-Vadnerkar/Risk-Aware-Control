INTERPRETATION: WHAT THE RESULTS TELL US

The mechanism holds for a specific subtype, not uniformly:
  → traffic_light_discrepancy dominates ONLY for genuine negative-evidence
    faults (tl_unknown, tl_blackout — perception reports nothing/unknown)
  → NOT for faults where perception reports a present-but-wrong value
    (tl_oscillate, confidence-ramp)
  → This is a sharper, more defensible version of the claim, not a weaker
    one — but it is a refinement, not a new contribution on its own

Calibration and lead time are directionally working, with real gaps:
  → Clean positive results exist (imu_fault_s3, imu_fault_stuck: 100%
    detection, real lead time, on genuinely dangerous trials)
  → The negative control (imu_fault_s1) correctly shows a low detection rate
  → TL faults have produced zero dangerous outcomes in this dataset so far —
    lead time is literally undefined for TL faults right now; this is the
    most important open gap, not a modeling failure

What this means for direction:
  → Lead time is supporting evidence for the calibrated-confidence claim,
    not the centerpiece — treating it as the headline risks narrowing the
    dissertation back toward a single "beat MRM by N seconds" number, which
    has limited value once Autoware's own MRM is already fast to react
  → The more defensible framing: signal quality and guarantee tightness
    first (this session's actual focus), lead time and severity as one
    supporting axis of that, not the whole claim
