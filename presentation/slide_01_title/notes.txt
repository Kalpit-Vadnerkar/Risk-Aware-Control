Title kept identical to prior presentations — the dissertation's core is
AI safety in AVs via digital twins, and that has not changed across any of
the reframes. The subtitle is what changed, again, since the last meeting:
"belief-divergence fault detection" is retired language (that framing
assumed a fixed set of known fault classes to detect and classify — see
slide 3). The subtitle now says what the work actually is: a calibrated
uncertainty signal (Layer 1, the bulk of this update) that a later stage
(Layer 2, next-steps) will translate into physical consequence. If asked
"didn't the title used to say fault detection" — yes, and the reframe is
itself part of the story worth being upfront about: the project moved from
"which known fault is this" to "how far off-nominal am I, and does that
matter," because the former can't say anything about a failure mode nobody
thought to name in advance, and doesn't need labeled fault data to work at
all for its first stage. This is a maturation of the claim, not a change of
subject or a retreat from a result that didn't pan out.
