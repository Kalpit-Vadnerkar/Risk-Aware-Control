This slide's narrative arc is deliberate: show the good result first
(reliability + efficiency), let it look convincing, THEN show exactly how
looking at one specific concrete example (not a random one — the single
sharpest turn in the dataset) surfaced a real problem the aggregate
numbers hid. This mirrors how the finding was actually made this cycle —
the visual trust-example plots (built specifically because "show me a
concrete example, not just statistics" was asked for) are what surfaced
this, not the aggregate numbers, which looked fine on their own.

If asked "so was the reliability diagram wrong" — no, and be precise
here: it's not wrong, it's an AVERAGE-CASE statement. It pools every
window, fold, and horizon step into one number per confidence level. The
turn-anticipation gap is a real, specific, findable pattern that a pooled
average can hide via cancellation (turns being worse is compensated for
by calm driving being better than needed) without the pooled number ever
being false. This distinction (pooled/average validity vs. per-scenario
validity) is the thread the rest of this section pulls on.

The efficiency-curve elbow finding is small but worth having ready as a
concrete, defensible answer if asked "why 90%, why not 95% or 99%" — it's
not an arbitrary choice, the data itself shows diminishing returns
sharply past that point for the traffic-light features specifically.
