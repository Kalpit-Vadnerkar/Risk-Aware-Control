This plot (experiments/scripts/plot_predictions.py) went through two real bugs before looking like this: (1) the goal marker was originally included in the plot's bounding-box calculation, which shrank the actual trajectory down to a barely-visible dot — fixed by excluding the goal from the bbox; (2) uncertainty circles drawn at every single timestep overlapped into an unreadable blob — fixed by drawing a sparse subset (~6 per trajectory) with outlined-only circles.

If asked to walk through the math live: position_out = clamp((point.x - ref_x) / 100, -1, 1), clamp((point.y - ref_y) / 100, -1, 1), where ref_x/ref_y is the ego position at the LAST past timestep (t=now) — so past-window positions read as "how far was I before now" and future-window positions read as "how far will I be after now," both anchored to the same present moment, with one shared scale across every example in the dataset (unlike the old per-window bbox scaling, which made position's units inconsistent from one training example to the next and was invisible to the model itself).

Good moment to connect back to slide 6's calibration numbers concretely: this is the plot those std(z) numbers are describing — position's uncertainty band is a bit too wide relative to its actual error (mildly underconfident), while traffic_light_state's band is a bit too narrow (overconfident) — visible directly if you compare how often the actual future line stays inside vs. outside the shaded band across several example windows.
