This plot (experiments/scripts/plot_predictions.py) went through two real bugs before looking like this: (1) the goal marker was originally included in the plot's bounding-box calculation, which shrank the actual trajectory down to a barely-visible dot — fixed by excluding the goal from the bbox; (2) uncertainty circles drawn at every single timestep overlapped into an unreadable blob — fixed by drawing a sparse subset (~6 per trajectory) with outlined-only circles.

If asked to walk through the math live: position_out = clamp((point.x - ref_x) / 100, -1, 1), clamp((point.y - ref_y) / 100, -1, 1), where ref_x/ref_y is the ego position at the LAST past timestep (t=now) — so past-window positions read as "how far was I before now" and future-window positions read as "how far will I be after now," both anchored to the same present moment, with one shared scale across every example in the dataset (unlike the old per-window bbox scaling, which made position's units inconsistent from one training example to the next and was invisible to the model itself).

UPDATED 2026-08-06 — the calibration story on this slide needs correcting. The old framing ("position mildly underconfident, velocity/steering/tl_state mildly-to-moderately overconfident," from slide 6's std(z) numbers) understated the real problem. Two new figures added this session, both computed on the held-out calibration split, both more rigorous than a single std(z) number:

coverage_curves.png — for each Gaussian head, nominal confidence level (x) vs. empirical coverage (y). A well-calibrated model tracks the diagonal. All 6 heads overshoot it at every level (max gap 0.156-0.275) — the predicted intervals are systematically too wide. Quantified the shape directly: excess kurtosis 4.8-8.0 for position/velocity/steering/acceleration, 44-116(!) for the two TL heads, against 0 for a true Gaussian; Anderson-Darling statistic 94-742 against a critical value of 0.79 — normality is decisively rejected for every single feature, not a borderline call. std(z) landing near 1.0 was hiding this: it can't tell "right variance, right shape" from "right variance, wrong shape," and this model is the latter.

horizon_widening.png — predicted std vs. actual RMSE across the 3-second prediction horizon. Slide 6's notes claim beta-NLL reweighting (Seitzer et al. 2022) already fixed the "variance doesn't widen at longer horizons" pathology — true only for position. For velocity, steering, acceleration, and both TL heads, predicted std stays nearly flat (some even dip before rising) while actual RMSE grows monotonically, reaching 2-4x the claimed uncertainty by t=3s. So the beta-NLL fix is real but incomplete — it's not clear yet whether that's an architecture issue (variance heads not conditioning on horizon step correctly) or something more specific to those five heads.

This is now the acknowledged blocking priority — see slide 11's update for why it cascades directly into the SPRT signal's behavior, and docs/research_notes/trust_and_signal_behavior_2026-08-06.md for the full writeup.
