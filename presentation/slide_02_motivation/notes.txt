The concrete reframe: the T-ITS 2025 paper reported 93.7% fault detection accuracy and identified Traffic Light Status Flag as the single most important feature (29.7% importance) with TL Status Flag x CUSUM as the top feature-residual combination (22%). Those numbers say the mechanism works — they say nothing about whether a specific flagged divergence should be trusted, or how far in advance it fires relative to when the fault actually becomes dangerous. That's the gap this chapter closes: conformal calibration (a distribution-free coverage guarantee, not an accuracy number) plus a lead-time measurement against a real fatal-moment anchor, plus a severity axis (does the guarantee hold as faults get worse, or only for mild ones).

Framing this for Dr. Pisu: this is the same move risk-assessment practice makes going from "we can detect anomalies" to "we can state a false-alarm rate and a time-to-detection with a guarantee" — the second is what a safety case actually needs, and it's the part the prior paper didn't attempt.
