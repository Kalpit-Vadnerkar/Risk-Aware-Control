This slide's job is unchanged from last time — set up why "it detects
faults accurately" isn't the finish line — but the destination it points
to has changed. Previously this led to a belief-divergence detector with
lead-time claims; now it leads to a calibrated-uncertainty signal (Layer
1) that a second stage will translate into physical consequence (Layer
2). Say plainly if asked: the underlying intuition "confidently wrong is
worse than honestly unsure" never changed — what changed is HOW we get to
"honestly unsure" (conformal calibration, a distribution-free statistical
guarantee) instead of a hand-built divergence signal, and WHAT we do with
it (roll it forward into consequence, not just alarm on it).

If asked why the destination changed: the belief-divergence framing
needed a fixed catalog of "what should the map/perception agree on," which
only ever covered the traffic-light channel well and required knowing in
advance which fault types to look for. Conformal calibration needs neither
— it gives a coverage guarantee on the model's OWN point predictions
directly, works for every predicted feature, and needs no labeled fault
data to build at all (only real faults to VALIDATE it against, which is
this update's newest result — see slide 13).
