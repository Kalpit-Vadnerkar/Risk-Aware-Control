THE PROBLEM: DETECTING A FAULT IS NOT THE SAME AS TRUSTING THE DETECTOR

Our prior work (T-ITS 2025) showed a digital twin CAN detect sensor faults
(camera, IMU, LiDAR) from behavioral residuals with high accuracy.

But accuracy alone does not answer the questions a safety case needs:
  → How confident should we be in a given flagged divergence — a calibrated
    number, not just "the residual went up"?
  → How many seconds of warning does the signal give before the fault
    actually becomes dangerous — is there time to act?
  → Does that warning degrade gracefully as the fault gets more severe,
    or does it fail unpredictably past some point?

None of these are answered by a detection-accuracy number, no matter how high.
A monitor that is confidently wrong is worse than one that is honestly unsure —
this is the actual bar a verification layer has to clear.

The reframed question:
  → Not "can the twin detect faults" (already shown)
  → But "can the twin's divergence signal be trusted with a stated guarantee,
    with enough lead time to matter, across a range of fault severities"

This is a verification claim, not a detection claim — and it is the actual
gap the dissertation is closing.
