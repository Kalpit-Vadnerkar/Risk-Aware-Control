THE PROBLEM: A MODEL CAN BE ACCURATE AND STILL NOT BE TRUSTWORTHY

Our prior work (T-ITS 2025) showed a digital twin can detect sensor faults
(camera, IMU, LiDAR) from behavioral residuals with high accuracy.

But "accurate on average" does not answer the questions a real safety
case needs:
  → When the model reports a prediction, how confident should we actually
    be — a number with a real guarantee, or just "the residual went up"?
  → Does that confidence estimate hold up EVERYWHERE, or only on the easy
    majority of driving (and quietly fail exactly where it matters most —
    turns, intersections)?
  → If something goes wrong with a sensor, does the model's own
    uncertainty actually notice — and does it notice in a way we can act on?

None of these are answered by a detection-accuracy number, no matter how
high. A model that is confidently wrong is worse than one that is
honestly unsure — that is the actual bar a trustworthy system has to clear.

The reframed question:
  → Not "can the twin detect faults" (already shown, and detecting a
    fault you already have a name for is the easy version of the problem)
  → But "does the twin know what it doesn't know, with a real guarantee,
    everywhere it drives — and does that self-knowledge respond correctly
    when something actually goes wrong"

This is a calibration and verification claim, not a detection claim — and
closing it properly is what this update is about.
