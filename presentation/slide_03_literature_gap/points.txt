LITERATURE GAP: CALIBRATED CONFIDENCE + LEAD TIME, NOT ANOTHER DETECTOR

Body 1 — AV fault/anomaly detection (well-studied, including our own prior work):
  → Residual-based, learned, and rule-based fault detectors for AV sensors
  → Reports accuracy / F1 / feature importance on a fixed test set
  → Representative: our T-ITS 2025 paper; broader anomaly-detection literature

Body 2 — Trajectory prediction for AVs (well-studied, actively moving fast):
  → VectorNet, LaneGCN, HiVT, Wayformer, QCNet — architecture-focused,
    optimized for point/multimodal accuracy, not calibrated uncertainty
  → Heteroscedastic regression pitfalls (Seitzer et al., beta-NLL, ICLR 2022)
    are directly relevant to this project's own model — see below

Body 3 — Conformal prediction / distribution-free calibration (active, growing):
  → Strong theory for i.i.d. or exchangeable calibration/test splits
  → Lindemann et al. (2023) connects conformal bounds to control decisions —
    closest precedent for turning a calibrated bound into a safety claim
  → Rarely applied to a map-grounded AV fault signal with a lead-time claim

The gap — calibrated, map-grounded, lead-time-aware fault verification:
  → No framework combines: (a) a mechanistic, map-grounded divergence signal,
    (b) a distribution-free confidence guarantee on that signal, and
    (c) a measured, severity-dependent lead time against a real safety outcome
  → This is what Stage 4 (conformal calibration + lead time) is built to close
