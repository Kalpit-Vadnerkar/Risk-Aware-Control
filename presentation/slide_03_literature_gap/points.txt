LITERATURE GAP: TWO TRADITIONS THAT DON'T TALK TO EACH OTHER

Tradition 1 — Uncertainty quantification / anomaly detection:
  → Monitors a model's own uncertainty, raises a flag past a threshold
  → Treats uncertainty as an ALARM MAGNITUDE — a number to threshold, not
    a quantity to reason further about
  → Split-conformal prediction for AV trajectory forecasting exists here
    (e.g. CUQDS) — but calibrates GLOBALLY (pooled), never checks whether
    that guarantee holds per real-world scenario

Tradition 2 — Risk-aware planning / reachability:
  → Computes physical consequence (Waymo's "Field of Safe Motion," 2026:
    forward reachability sets, a collision-free "out" at every instant)
  → ASSUMES its own prediction/state estimate is correct — doesn't
    question whether the input it's propagating is trustworthy right now

Neither tradition alone answers: "my model is uncertain in this SPECIFIC
way, right now — therefore this specific driving behavior is unsafe, by
THIS much." That translation is the actual gap.

The closest prior work found (Reuter et al., ITSC 2026) — genuinely
close, worth knowing precisely how this differs:
  → Also finds pooled conformal coverage hides a per-scenario gap on a
    real AV (curvature-based grouping)
  → But: ONE binary grouping variable (not real map-zone categories),
    fixes the gap only by RECALIBRATING (never retrains the model), a
    single scalar output (not a 7-feature trajectory), synthetic actuator
    degradation (not injected sensor/perception faults), no comparison
    against a continuous/embedding alternative
